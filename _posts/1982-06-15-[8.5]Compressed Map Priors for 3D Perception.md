# [8.5] Compressed Map Priors for 3D Perception

- Authors: Brady Zhou, Philipp Kr\"ahenb\"uhl
- [arXiv Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00139)
- [PDF Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00139.pdf)

## Subfields
 3D Perception / Map Priors (3D感知 / 地图先验)
## Reason for Interest

Innovation: The paper proposes a novel 'Compressed Map Prior' (CMP) using multi-resolution hash encodings (similar to Instant NGP) combined with binary quantization. This allows for extremely efficient storage (32KB/km²) while maintaining high representational capacity.

Practicality: It addresses a core reality of AV deployment—fleets repeatedly traverse the same areas. The method is detector-agnostic and integrates end-to-end into major architectures (BEVDet, BEVFormer, PETR).

Experiments: The evaluation is rigorous, covering detection and segmentation tasks. It demonstrates consistent improvements across three different baseline architectures. The ablation studies (e.g., impact of camera dropout, binarization) strongly support the claims. While the absolute performance is compared in a single-frame setting (excluding temporal queues to isolate the prior's benefit), the relative gains and efficiency make it a high-value contribution to the mapping and perception literature.
## Abstract: 
Human drivers rarely travel where no person has gone before. After all, thousands of drivers use busy city roads every day, and only one can claim to be the first. The same holds for autonomous computer vision systems. The vast majority of the deployment area of an autonomous vision system will have been visited before. Yet, most autonomous vehicle vision systems act as if they are encountering each location for the first time. In this work, we present Compressed Map Priors (CMP), a simple but effective framework to learn spatial priors from historic traversals. The map priors use a binarized hashmap that requires only $32\text{KB}/\text{km}^2$, a $20\times$ reduction compared to the dense storage. Compressed Map Priors easily integrate into leading 3D perception systems at little to no extra computational costs, and lead to a significant and consistent improvement in 3D object detection on the nuScenes dataset across several architectures.
